## The First Step in Every Computer… and the Last Bastion of Nonfree Software

Every time you press your computer's power button, a program you never see starts working: the BIOS or UEFI. This program is responsible for initializing the hardware and handing control to the operating system. It exists in virtually every personal computer in the world, yet in the vast majority of cases it is **nonfree, closed-source software**. This means users cannot know what this code does, study it, modify it, or verify that it contains no backdoors.

On September 14, 2026, the Free Software Foundation (FSF) announced that the **GNU Boot** project has joined its Fiscal Sponsorship Program, a step aimed at strengthening the development of a libre, ethical alternative to this critical component.

🔗 **Official Announcement:** [fsf.org/news/2026-gnu-boot-joins-fsf-fiscal-sponsorship-program](https://www.fsf.org/news/2026-gnu-boot-joins-fsf-fiscal-sponsorship-program)
🔗 **GNU Boot Website:** [gnu.org/software/gnuboot](https://www.gnu.org/software/gnuboot/)

## What Is GNU Boot?

GNU Boot is **libre, ethical boot firmware** that aims to replace the nonfree BIOS and UEFI found in personal computers. The project was founded in 2022 by Denis "GNUtoo" Carikli and Adrien "neox" Bourmault.

The team's work is very similar to that of GNU/Linux distributions: they package software such as **Coreboot** and **GNU GRUB**, contribute to the upstream repositories of these projects, and assist them with documentation, among other tasks.

The long-term goal of GNU Boot is to allow users and contributors to rely on GNU Boot without requiring them to learn domain-specific skills. In this regard, the project takes **GNU Guix** and **Trisquel** as its models. As with GNU Guix, GNU Boot also focuses on **software reproducibility**.

## Why Is This Merger Important?

### Legitimacy and Institutional Support
GNU Boot joining the FSF's Fiscal Sponsorship Program gives it a legal and institutional standing without needing to incorporate on its own. This means the team can focus on technical development instead of administrative and financial matters.

### Trust and Reputation
The Free Software Foundation, founded in 1985, is the most established organization in defending software freedom. GNU Boot joining its projects strengthens the trust of users, donors, and the technical community.

### Financial Sustainability
Projects in the Fiscal Sponsorship Program gain increased awareness and donations, and benefit from the FSF's fundraising and management capacity. This means GNU Boot will receive more stable funding for the development of free boot firmware.

## Official Statements

> "The BIOS is a delicate but essential part of software freedom for computing today. GNU Boot aims to make sure that we can continue using our computers in freedom." — **Zoë Kooyman**, Executive Director of the Free Software Foundation.

> "Making this decision with the FSF allows us to focus more on what we do best: writing and debugging code that keeps our machines fully free." — **Adrien Bourmault**, co-maintainer of GNU Boot.

## Other Projects in the Program

GNU Boot is not the first project to join the FSF's Fiscal Sponsorship Program. Other projects represented in the program include:

- **GNU Toolchain:** The core development toolchain (GCC, Binutils, GDB, Glibc).
- **GNU Guix:** A fully free package manager and Linux distribution, with a focus on reproducibility.
- **EmacsConf:** The annual conference for the Emacs community.

These projects share a commitment to serving the FSF's mission and advancing users' freedom in computing.

## What Does This Mean for the Average User?

To the average user, the topic of boot firmware may seem distant from daily life. But it is, in fact, **the foundation of everything**. If the BIOS is nonfree, then one cannot fully trust that the computer is working in the user's interest and not that of the manufacturer or any other party.

GNU Boot, especially after joining the FSF, represents a step toward a computer where every line of code can be verified, from the moment the power button is pressed to the moment the system shuts down. This is the essence of the "software freedom" idea that the FSF has defended since 1985.

## Summary

GNU Boot joining the Free Software Foundation's Fiscal Sponsorship Program is important news for the free software community. It strengthens a vital project that aims to liberate the very first step in running any computer: the boot firmware. With the FSF's support, the GNU Boot team can focus on development, improve reproducibility, and bring the libre alternative to BIOS and UEFI within reach of more users.
